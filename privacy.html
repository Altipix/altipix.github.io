Stackly Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 8, 2026

Stackly (the "game") is published by Altipix ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the game collects, why, who receives it, and the choices you have. You can contact us at altipixsupport@gmail.com.

The short version

  • There is no Stackly account. We never ask for your name, email address or phone number.
  • Your progress is saved on your phone. If you sign in with Google Play Games, a copy is also saved in your own Google account so it can follow you to a new phone, and your scores appear on the leaderboards.
  • If you tap Allow on the stats question, we collect anonymous play statistics. If you tap No thanks, we do not.
  • The game shows ads through Google AdMob. These use your device's advertising ID.
  • The game does not use your contacts, camera, microphone or photos, and does not ask for your precise location.
  • The game is for people aged 13 and over.

What the game collects, and why

1. Saved on your device (we cannot see it). Your best score, coins, unlocked themes, which locked themes you have already tried, chosen theme, daily streak, sound, vibration, stats and reminder settings, whether you have seen the tips, and counters for how many reward ads you have used. It is stored in an encrypted file in the game's private storage. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data deletes it.

2. Google Play Games (only if you sign in). If you choose to sign in, the game uses Google Play Games Services, a Google service. Through it:

  • Your best score and your best daily streak are sent to the game's leaderboards. Leaderboards show scores together with your Play Games profile name and picture, so other players can see them.
  • A small cloud save is stored in your Google account: your best score, coins, unlocked themes, which locked themes you have already tried, chosen theme, streak, number of rounds played and whether you have seen the tips. It lets the game restore your progress on a new phone. We cannot read it ourselves; it is stored by Google for you.

Google also collects some information through Play Games Services itself, such as your gamer profile (name and picture) and technical data about how the service performs. You can control or delete your Play Games profile and its game data in the Google Play Games app or your Google Account. Google's own privacy policy applies to this data: https://policies.google.com/privacy

3. Anonymous play statistics (only if you tap Allow). We use Google Firebase Analytics. We record events such as: a round started, ended or was abandoned (with the score), a power-up was used, a theme was bought (with its price, and whether you had tried it first), a locked theme was tried after watching an ad, the shop or the leaderboard was opened, streak events, whether an ad was shown or completed, and whether a cloud save succeeded. Firebase also records some information automatically: an anonymous app-instance ID (a random identifier that Firebase creates for each installation of the game; it is not your advertising ID, it is not linked to your name or email, and it is reset if you uninstall and reinstall the game), your device model and operating system version, app version, language, and your approximate location (such as city or region, worked out from your internet connection). We do not receive your name or email. You can turn this off at any time in Settings > Share stats; collection then stops. If you tap No thanks, the game keeps Firebase Analytics switched off, sends no statistics events, and Firebase does not fetch its analytics app-instance ID. Firebase may still create a random installation ID on your phone, but it is not used to collect statistics while Analytics is off.

4. Ads (Google AdMob). The game shows a full-screen ad every few rounds, a small banner ad at the bottom of the Themes page, and optional ads you can choose to watch for a reward (for example free coins, or to try a locked theme for one round). AdMob, which is part of Google, and its advertising partners may use your device's advertising ID, your IP address (from which a general location can be estimated) and technical information about your device (such as device type, operating system, how you interact with the game and with ads, and app performance information like launch time) to show and measure ads and to prevent fraud. This happens whether or not you allowed statistics. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and some US states, a privacy message asks about your ad choices before ads are shown. You can reset your advertising ID or turn off ad personalisation in your phone's settings (Settings > Google > Ads, or Settings > Privacy > Ads). On Android versions that support them, Google's ad system can also use Android's Privacy Sandbox ad services, for example Topics (a few broad interest categories chosen by your phone, shared with advertisers instead of an individual ID) and attribution reporting (measuring whether an ad led to an install or an action). You can control these in your phone's privacy or ads settings (the menu name differs between phones).

5. Time check. To stop the daily streak from being faked by changing the phone's clock, the game asks Google (https://www.google.com) for the current date. The game makes the same kind of request when you open the Leaderboard, to check that you are online. Google can see your IP address like any web request. Nothing else is sent.

6. Reminders. If you turn on the Reminder in Settings, the game schedules a notification on your phone. It needs your permission to show notifications. No information is sent to us for this.

7. Feedback email. If you tap Send feedback, your own email app opens with our address filled in. We receive only what you choose to send.

8. Permissions. The game uses internet access and network state (ads, Play Games, statistics, the time check), vibration (haptic feedback), the advertising ID, notifications and restart handling (for the optional reminder), and wake lock (so a reminder can appear). It also declares, through Google's ad and analytics libraries, the Android ad services permissions (so Google's ad system can use the Privacy Sandbox features described in section 4), the Google Play install referrer permission (which lets those tools learn which Play Store link led to an install) and a foreground service permission (used for background work inside the libraries the game is built on). It does not ask for your location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos or files.

How we use it

To run the game, save and restore your progress, show leaderboards, keep the streak and leaderboards fair, show ads that pay for the game, understand how the game is used so we can improve it (statistics only if you agree), and respond to your messages. We do not sell your personal information for money and we do not use it for profiling that affects you in any significant way.

Legal bases (for people in the EEA, UK and Switzerland)

  • Consent: anonymous statistics and personalised ads (you can withdraw at any time as described above).
  • Contract: providing the game and, if you sign in, your leaderboard and cloud save.
  • Legitimate interests: keeping the game working and fair (the time check, preventing fraud) and responding to your messages.

How long we keep it

  • On your device: until you delete the game or its data.
  • Google Analytics: event data and the anonymous user identifier are each kept for up to 14 months. After that, Google deletes them automatically.
  • Play Games scores and cloud save: kept by Google until you delete them (see "Your choices").
  • Emails you send us: kept for as long as needed to deal with your message.

Who receives it

We do not share your information with anyone except the service providers the game is built on, who handle it for the purposes described above:

  • Google (Play Games Services, Firebase Analytics, AdMob, and the time check) and, for ads, Google's advertising partners.
  • Other players see your leaderboard entries and Play Games profile name and picture, if you sign in.
  • Authorities, if the law requires it.

Your choices

  • Statistics: Settings > Share stats.
  • Ads: use the privacy message shown in your region, or reset your advertising ID, turn off ad personalisation, or change the Ads and Privacy Sandbox settings in your phone's settings.
  • Reminders: Settings > Reminder, or the phone's notification settings.
  • Play Games and cloud save: you can sign out of Play Games and delete your Play Games data and game progress in the Google Play Games app or your Google Account settings.
  • Everything on your device: uninstall the game or clear its data.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access your information, correct it, delete it, object to or restrict how it is used, withdraw consent, receive a copy, and, in the EEA and UK, complain to your data protection authority. Some US states (including California, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas, Utah and Virginia) give similar rights, including the right to opt out of "sharing" or targeted advertising. Because we do not have accounts, names or emails, we usually cannot tell which data belongs to you; anonymous statistics are linked only to an anonymous ID on your phone, which you can give us if you want us to try to find it. To make a request, email altipixsupport@gmail.com. We will reply as soon as we reasonably can, and within the time the law requires. We do not discriminate against you for using these rights.

Children

The game is for people 13 and over. It is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child under 13 has given us information, tell us and we will help get it removed. Where your country sets a higher age for consent, do not use the game's optional features without a parent or guardian's permission.

International transfers

Google processes information in the United States and other countries, which may have different data protection laws from yours. Google describes the safeguards it uses in its own terms and policies.

Security

Your saved progress is stored in an encrypted file on your phone, and we use well-known Google services for the rest. No system is perfectly secure, so we cannot guarantee absolute security.

Changes to this policy

If we change how the game handles information, we will update this page and the date at the top. If a change is significant, we will also tell you in the game or on the store page where required.
